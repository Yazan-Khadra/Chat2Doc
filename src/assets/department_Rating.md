# 1. Characteristics of an Organized Company

| # | Characteristic | What it means | Indicator (observable condition) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Clear purpose and strategic alignment** | Mission, vision, and goals are defined and cascade down to departments and teams. | Every department can state how its work contributes to company goals. Goals are written, measurable, and time-bound. |
| 2 | **Defined organizational structure** | Departments, reporting lines, and boundaries are explicit and logical. | An up-to-date org chart exists. Each person has one clear line of reporting, and spans of control are reasonable. |
| 3 | **Clear roles and responsibilities** | Every task and decision has an identifiable owner, with no gaps or overlaps. | Job descriptions and RACI-style ownership exist. No one is unsure who is responsible for a given deliverable. |
| 4 | **Standardized, documented processes** | Core workflows are written down, owned, and followed. | Key processes have SOPs with an owner, a version, and a review date. Results do not vary significantly between people doing the same task. |
| 5 | **Clear governance and decision-making** | Decision rights, approvals, and escalation paths are defined. | Staff know who decides what, when to escalate, and how long decisions take. Decisions are recorded. |
| 6 | **Effective communication and information flow** | Information reaches the right people at the right time through defined channels. | Regular meeting and reporting rhythms exist. There is a single source of truth for shared information. Handoffs between teams are defined. |
| 7 | **Performance measurement and accountability** | Performance is tracked against targets using agreed indicators. | KPIs exist per department and role, are reviewed regularly, and lead to action when off target. |
| 8 | **Competent, well-managed people** | Recruitment, onboarding, training, and evaluation follow a consistent approach. | New members reach productivity through a defined onboarding path. Skills match role requirements. Evaluations are periodic and criteria-based. |
| 9 | **Quality control and continuous improvement** | The company checks its own work and learns from problems. | Reviews or audits happen on a schedule. Issues get root-cause analysis and corrective actions, and those actions are tracked to closure. |
| 10 | **Managed knowledge and documentation** | Knowledge is captured, stored, and retrievable rather than held by individuals. | Documents are organized, versioned, and access-controlled. Losing one person does not stop a process. |
| 11 | **Integration across departments** | Departments operate as one system, not isolated units. | Interfaces between departments (inputs, outputs, timing) are defined. Cross-team conflicts and duplicated work are rare. |
| 12 | **Risk awareness and adaptability** | The company anticipates problems and can change deliberately. | Key risks are identified with owners. Changes to processes or structure follow a defined path and are communicated. |

## 4. Clarity Criteria

An organizational element is considered **clear** when the people who need to understand or act on it can do so without guessing, unnecessary clarification, or relying on informal knowledge. Clarity means that the element has a defined purpose and scope, clear ownership, sufficient detail, accessible documentation, consistent interpretation, and a practical way to verify whether it is being applied correctly.

These criteria can be applied to any organizational element, including **departments, roles, responsibilities, processes, workflows, KPIs, policies, decision rights, structures, and documents**. The purpose is not only to verify whether an element exists, but whether it is sufficiently defined and understood to support consistent organizational work.

|  # | Clarity Criterion | The Element Is Clear When...                                                                          | Ambiguity Indicator                                                                                   |
| -: | ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|  1 | **Defined**       | Its purpose, scope, boundaries, and intended function are clearly stated.                             | Purpose is assumed or implied; boundaries are unclear.                                                |
|  2 | **Specific**      | It uses precise and concrete language that allows one reasonable interpretation.                      | Vague terms such as “soon,” “as needed,” or “appropriate” are used without criteria.                  |
|  3 | **Owned**         | One clearly identified person or role is accountable for it.                                          | No owner exists, ownership is shared without a lead, or responsibility is assigned to “the team.”     |
|  4 | **Complete**      | Relevant inputs, outputs, responsibilities, steps, conditions, and known exceptions are covered.      | Important information is missing and people have to fill the gaps themselves.                         |
|  5 | **Documented**    | It is formally documented, approved, accessible, and maintained in an appropriate location.           | It exists mainly through verbal instructions, individual knowledge, or conflicting documents.         |
|  6 | **Understood**    | The people affected by it can explain and apply it correctly in their own words.                      | Different people interpret or describe it differently.                                                |
|  7 | **Consistent**    | It aligns with related roles, processes, policies, structures, and objectives.                        | Different documents or organizational elements contradict each other or create overlapping authority. |
|  8 | **Verifiable**    | Its application or success can be checked through defined evidence, criteria, or measures.            | There is no clear way to demonstrate whether it has been applied correctly or successfully.           |
|  9 | **Current**       | It has a valid version, approval/review date, and reflects the organization's current way of working. | It is outdated, undated, or no longer reflects actual practice.                                       |
| 10 | **Actionable**    | A person can act on it directly without needing additional interpretation or informal clarification.  | Work is delayed, clarification is repeatedly required, or employees depend on workarounds.            |

### 4 Critical Clarity Criteria

Three criteria are considered **critical**:

* **Defined** — people must know what the element is and what it covers.
* **Owned** — accountability must be clearly assigned.
* **Understood** — the people affected must interpret it consistently.

If an element fails any of these three criteria, it should **not be considered fully clear**, even if it performs well against the other criteria.

### Practical Assessment Tests

| Test                 | Purpose                                                          | Assessment Question                                                                           |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Two-Person Test**  | Check consistency of understanding.                              | Can two people independently describe the same element in substantially the same way?         |
| **New-Joiner Test**  | Check whether the documentation is sufficient for practical use. | Could a new employee understand and apply it using the available documentation?               |
| **Absence Test**     | Identify dependency on individuals.                              | Could the work continue if the current owner were unavailable for an extended period?         |
| **Ownership Test**   | Verify accountability.                                           | Can the organization clearly answer: “Who is accountable if this fails?”                      |
| **Evidence Test**    | Distinguish documented claims from actual evidence.              | Can the organization provide a document, record, output, or observation supporting the claim? |
| **Recent-Case Test** | Compare defined practice with actual practice.                   | Can people provide a recent real example showing how the element was actually applied?        |

### Clarity Rating

| Rating              | Meaning                                      | General Condition                                                                            |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Clear**           | Sufficiently defined and understood.         | Meets the core criteria with no significant impact on action or consistency.                 |
| **Partially Clear** | Exists but contains gaps or inconsistencies. | Some criteria are not fully met, causing occasional confusion or variation.                  |
| **Unclear**         | Ambiguous, informal, or disorganized.        | One or more critical criteria are not met, or several other criteria are significantly weak. |
| **Absent**          | Not defined.                                 | No meaningful definition, ownership, or documentation exists.                                |

The criteria should be used as an **assessment framework**, not simply as a documentation checklist. The objective is to determine whether each organizational element is sufficiently clear to support **consistent understanding, ownership, execution, and accountability across the organization**.

## 5 Range of Work

The assessment will examine the organization at multiple levels, from the company as a whole down to individual tasks. This allows the assessment to identify gaps not only within each level, but also in the relationships and alignment between them.

|  # | Assessment Level                     | What Will Be Assessed & Analyzed                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -: | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
|  1 | **Company**                          | The overall organizational direction, structure, objectives, operating model, governance, communication, resources, performance, and alignment across the organization.                                                              |
|  2 | **Departments**                      | The purpose, structure, responsibilities, objectives, resources, workflows, processes, performance measures, and relationships of each department with the rest of the organization.                                                 |
|  3 | **Department Managers**              | The manager's responsibilities, authority, decision-making scope, accountability, management practices, coordination responsibilities, and alignment with departmental objectives.                                                   |
|  4 | **Roles Within Each Department**     | The roles required by each department, including job descriptions, responsibilities, authority, competencies, reporting lines, expected outputs, and boundaries between roles.                                                       |
|  5 | **Processes Within Each Department** | The processes and workflows used by each department, including purpose, inputs, outputs, ownership, sequence, dependencies, controls, handoffs, performance, and improvement opportunities.                                          |
|  6 | **Tasks**                            | Individual tasks performed within roles and processes, including task ownership, purpose, inputs, outputs, dependencies, execution steps, required resources, expected standards, and relationship to the relevant role and process. |

### Assessment Relationship

**Company → Department → Department Manager → Role → Process → Task**

This structure allows each lower level to be assessed in relation to the level above it, ensuring that **tasks support processes, processes support roles and departments, and departments support the overall company objectives and direction**.

# Box 2 — Structure

## Organizational Architecture

Growth Wizzards uses a **Hub-and-Spoke organizational architecture**.

The structure separates:

1. **Strategic decision-making**
2. **Value management**
3. **Functional capability ownership**
4. **Project delivery**
5. **Project coordination**
6. **External and internal execution**

The organization does **not** operate as a traditional hierarchy in which every employee permanently belongs to one project.

Instead:

> **Departments own capabilities, while projects consume and combine those capabilities according to business needs.**

This creates a **Dynamic Resource Matrix** in which people and AI resources can participate in different projects according to their competence, experience, reliability, and required level of autonomy.

# Organizational Layers

## 1. Core — Steering Team

The **Steering Team** is the strategic center of Growth Wizzards.

It is responsible for:

* Strategic direction
* Major organizational decisions
* Company-wide priorities
* Overall development of Growth Wizzards

The Steering Team determines **where the organization is going and what should receive strategic attention**.

---

## 2. Ring 1 — VMO

The **Value Management Office (VMO)** is the value-management and prioritization layer.

It evaluates initiatives before they enter execution and ensures that organizational resources are directed toward initiatives that create sufficient value.

```text
Strategic Intent
       ↓
     VMO
       ↓
Value Assessment
       ↓
Prioritization
       ↓
Execution
```

The VMO therefore connects **strategic decisions** with **resource allocation and execution priorities**.

---

## 3. Ring 2 — Departments

Departments are the organization's **permanent capability structures**.

They own and develop specialized knowledge, standards, expertise, and professional capabilities.

### Current Department

**Technical Department**

The Technical Department owns the technical capabilities required by Growth Wizzards and its projects.

Departments are **not project teams**.

Instead:

> **Departments provide the capabilities from which project teams are assembled.**

---

# 4. Ring 3 — Delivery Unit

The **Delivery Unit** is the organization's stable execution structure.

Its purpose is to convert approved business needs into measurable outcomes.

The Delivery Unit is primarily composed of:

### Wizzards

Core members who are fully integrated into Growth Wizzards and can independently own work and make decisions within their authority.

### Supporting-Wizzards

Contributors who can independently execute defined work but have limited availability or are still progressing toward full organizational integration.

### AI Agents

Autonomous or semi-autonomous systems that perform defined work or support workflows under human supervision.

The Delivery Unit therefore represents the organization's **core execution capacity**.

---

# 5. Ring 4 — PMO

The **PMO** is the operational coordination layer between organizational capabilities and project execution.

It is responsible for:

* Project coordination
* Operational planning
* Project tracking
* Execution coordination
* Supporting project teams

The PMO can include:

* **Wizzards**
* **Supporting-Wizzards**
* **Trainees**

The PMO is therefore not a separate workforce category. It is a **functional operating layer that can be staffed by members of the Delivery Unit and developing contributors**.

---

# 6. Outer Ring — Projects

Projects are the organization's actual execution environments.

They may include:

* Internal projects
* New ventures
* Client work
* New-sector exploration

Projects do not have a permanently fixed workforce.

Instead, each project is assembled dynamically according to its requirements.

### Project Team Composition

A project may contain:

| Resource Type          | Role in Projects                                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Wizzard**            | Core owner, decision-maker, or senior contributor      |
| **Supporting-Wizzard** | Independent contributor with defined responsibility    |
| **Trainee**            | Contributor working under appropriate supervision      |
| **Support**            | External contributor performing specific defined tasks |
| **Freelancer**         | External specialist engaged for a specific need        |
| **AI Agent**           | AI-based execution or workflow-support resource        |

Therefore:

```text
                         PROJECT
                            │
             ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
             │              │              │
          Wizzard      Supporting-      AI Agent
                         Wizzard
             │              │              │
             ├──────────────┼──────────────┤
             │              │
          Trainee         Support
                            │
                         Freelancer
```

Not every project requires every resource type.

The composition depends on:

* Required capabilities
* Complexity
* Risk
* Required autonomy
* Available resources
* Project scope

# Workforce Roles

## Wizzard

A core member of Growth Wizzards who is fully integrated into the organization.

A Wizzard may operate across:

* Department capability work
* Delivery Unit
* PMO
* Project leadership
* Project execution

A Wizzard can therefore move between organizational layers according to the responsibility assigned.

---

## Supporting-Wizzard

A contributor who shares the company's values and can independently execute defined work but has limited availability or is still progressing toward full integration.

A Supporting-Wizzard primarily contributes through:

* Delivery
* PMO
* Project execution

---

## Trainee

A developing contributor who participates in defined work under appropriate supervision.

A Trainee primarily operates within:

* PMO
* Project teams

A Trainee does **not automatically hold independent project ownership or decision authority**.

---

## Support

An external or auxiliary contributor who performs clearly defined tasks without deep integration into Growth Wizzards' organizational structure.

Support resources are primarily attached to specific projects or operational needs.

---

## Freelancer

An external specialist engaged for:

* A specific project
* A specific task
* A defined period

Freelancers are project-based resources rather than permanent organizational members.

---

## AI Agent

An autonomous or semi-autonomous AI resource that can be assigned to:

* Repetitive execution
* Data processing
* Workflow automation
* Analysis
* Operational support
* Other defined project activities

AI Agents operate under appropriate **human ownership and supervision**.

## Structural Principle

The fundamental structural principle of Growth Wizzards is:

> **The organization owns capabilities; the Delivery Unit coordinates execution; PMO coordinates projects; and project teams are dynamically assembled from the available human and AI resources.**

This allows Growth Wizzards to maintain a **stable organizational core** while dynamically changing its execution capacity according to business requirements.
